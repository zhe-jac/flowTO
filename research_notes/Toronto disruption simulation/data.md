# Toronto data for disruption simulation

Verified 2026-10-17 using live City CKAN `package_show` and small `datastore_search` queries, plus direct HTTP probes. Catalogue modification/refresh timestamps do not mean every individual observation is recent.

## Which data can power a downtown-first prototype?

### Takeaway
Toronto supplies a useful free street backbone, reported restrictions, sampled traffic counts and TTC schedules/positions. Those are enough for an explainable scenario prototype; they do not constitute a complete, calibrated city traffic simulation.

### Inferences
- Use Centreline intersection IDs and direction fields to build a directed road graph; do not connect every geometric line crossing, particularly at bridges and underpasses. Filter actual drivable road classes. A display GeoJSON extract is not a verified routing graph.
- Match restrictions by geometry, road names, from/to roads and direction, with ambiguous matches reviewable. `High` impact does not mathematically specify a lane-capacity reduction.
- Begin with counts near the demo area, showing observation date and whether a speed/flow value is measured or inferred. Daily catalogue updates cannot make a 2019 observation into a 2026 baseline.
- TTC first supports affected route/stop detection and illustrative road delay. Full operational transit simulation also needs headways, stop dwell and dispatch/holding/detour assumptions beyond merely drawing vehicles.

### Gaps
- Complete lane counts, signal phases/timings, legal turn restrictions, actual capacity, current citywide speeds and time-dependent origin–destination (OD) demand were not established in these open inputs. Counts constrain flows but do not uniquely reveal complete trips or where displaced traffic will go.
- Road feed endpoint returned HTTP 200, but a full Python JSON parse failed with `Invalid \\escape` around character 608140 during the probe. Successful response status is not proof of usable payload. Validate parser behavior, consider CSV/XML fallback and cache the last valid snapshot. Exact duration/coverage of this transient data issue is unknown.

## What do accidents, events and free speed data actually provide?

### Takeaway
Represent an accident as a user-selected capacity reduction and duration, and an event as extra trips plus any explicit street closure. Historical collision and event-calendar data provide context; they do not directly supply live blockage duration or attendee travel demand.

### Cited Findings
- The [KSI collision dataset metadata](https://ckan0.cf.opendata.inter.prod-toronto.ca/api/3/action/package_show?id=motor-vehicle-collisions-involving-killed-or-seriously-injured-persons) describes serious/fatal motor-vehicle or streetcar collisions on City right-of-way since 2006, one row per involved person. It excludes private property and provincial highways. Despite daily file refresh, finalized records can arrive months to more than a year after a collision. Deduplicate collision IDs before counting incidents; this is safety/history context rather than live accident detection. [Public dataset](https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/motor-vehicle-collisions-involving-killed-or-seriously-injured-persons/).
- [Festivals & Events](https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/festivals-events/) is the approved City calendar, submitted by organizers rather than a complete list of all city events. [Metadata](https://ckan0.cf.opendata.inter.prod-toronto.ca/api/3/action/package_show?id=festivals-events) was refreshed 2026-10-16 and labels publication real-time; the [actual JSON](https://ckan0.cf.opendata.inter.prod-toronto.ca/dataset/9201059e-43ed-4369-885e-0b867652feac/resource/8900fdb2-7f6c-4f50-8581-b463311ff05d/download/file.json) includes recurring dates, descriptions and event locations, with empty coordinates in an inspected entry. The first record inspected was a 2025 event: filter dates and validate geocoding rather than assume every returned record is upcoming.
- [Travel Times—Bluetooth metadata](https://ckan0.cf.opendata.inter.prod-toronto.ca/api/3/action/package_show?id=travel-times-bluetooth) describes 5-minute sampled sensor travel times and claims historic plus real-time availability. The [City Transportation Data & Analytics source documentation](https://github.com/CityofToronto/bdit_data-sources#open-data-releases) explicitly says that real-time feed is not operational. Actual CKAN resources inspected were 2014–2017 archives plus route geometry, with old resource timestamps. Treat as historical only until a working newer feed is proven.
- [City data-source documentation](https://github.com/CityofToronto/bdit_data-sources#here-travel-time-data) describes HERE probe-derived link speeds used internally. The existence of public ingestion code does not grant public access to the underlying commercial dataset.
- TTC has a distinct [GTFS-RT service disruptions dataset](https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/ttc-gtfs-realtime-gtfs-rt/), whose [metadata](https://ckan0.cf.opendata.inter.prod-toronto.ca/api/3/action/package_show?id=ttc-gtfs-realtime-gtfs-rt) lists bus, streetcar, subway alerts, planned closures, reduced speed zones and accessibility outages. Its supplied endpoint `https://gtfsrt.ttc.ca` returned HTTP 403 to this research environment. Verify access and feed semantics separately before promising it in the demo.

### Inferences
- Start with explicit organizer inputs: attendance range, arrival/departure windows, driving/transit/walking shares and vehicles per party. Attendance does not equal car trips. Give low/base/high scenarios and let users see the assumptions.
- Accident type may alter blocked lanes, speed or turning permissions; historical collision frequency does not automatically tell the simulator which present-day link is blocked or when it will clear.
- Traffic animation should reflect model flow/speed output, while the UI labels it an estimate. An LLM can parse incident notices or a sentence describing an event, then request confirmation of uncertain structured fields; it should not invent traffic measurements.

### Gaps
- No verified free, comprehensive, live downtown speed surface was found in this targeted pass. SVC is a practical free observed-speed starting point, with sparse dates/sites; Mapbox traffic tiles or commercial routing are separate services/terms, not a source of arbitrary-network counterfactual simulation.
- Event attendance, origin distribution, mode share, parking/rideshare behavior, full restriction polygons and permit conditions were not guaranteed in the calendar feed.
- No evidence that the road-restriction feed captures every collision, all minor incidents or all provincial-road incidents. Preserve reported/observed versus simulated status.
