## Design Context

### Users

City officials, event organizers, residents, journalists, and community groups exploring Toronto road closures, construction, events, and transit impacts.

### Brand Personality

Readable, approachable, practical. The user prefers a light sky-blue theme and rejected the earlier brown palette. Use cool off-white land, sky-blue water and buildings, soft mint parks, and dark blue-grey labels.

### Aesthetic Direction

The user asked to remove the surrounding workspace and add features one at a time. Keep the map full-screen with minimal controls. Carry the light sky-blue direction through both map views and their controls. Keep the overall appearance bright and avoid brown or beige accents.

The user approved a distinct basemap theme and label cleanup first, followed by custom controls later. Hide business and tourist clutter while preserving street, neighbourhood, and transit context. Retain the 3D facades.

The user requested automatic night mode tied to simulator time. Use Toronto sunrise and sunset for the selected simulation date, with gradual dawn/dusk transitions into a navy basemap, blue glass surfaces, pale readable labels, matching boundary fade, and softer disruption-model lighting. Restore the light sky-blue palette during daytime without recreating the map. Keep traffic colours and motion consistent.

The user requested liquid glass throughout the interface. Use translucent sky-blue surfaces, backdrop blur, softly rounded corners, fine white rim highlights, and subtle SVG edge refraction. Keep text and icons sharp, closure accents coral, and the map canvas unfiltered. Provide opaque fallbacks and respect reduced-transparency and reduced-motion preferences.

The user found the 30%/52% tint trial too transparent and asked for slightly more opacity. Small controls now use 45% tint and reading panels 64%, with 8–10 px blur. The toolbox, 2D/3D switch, and zoom/compass controls must share the same `liquid-glass-react` rendering layer, border treatment, and corner radii. Keep the map and drag interactions independent of the optical renderer.

### Design Principles

- Make the map usable before adding surrounding interface.
- Preserve the working worker configuration and map initialization.
- Use colour to distinguish geographic features without implying calculated traffic impacts.
- Keep mode controls labelled, keyboard accessible, and visible over the map.
- Keep future closure and congestion indicators distinguishable from base-map styling.
- In 3D, automatically blend foreground buildings where they obstruct traffic overlays in a soft central viewing area and around the cursor while placing or resizing disruptions. Keep passive road corridors narrow and softer than cursor clearing. Preserve native detailed buildings and solid viewport edges elsewhere, vary the blend with pitch, and avoid adding a Reveal roads toggle. The desired behaviour follows Apple Maps overlay transparency and works during normal observation.

The bottom playback bar groups the clock with a compact date picker in place of the weekday/weekend toggle. Its glass calendar opens above the bar without clipping, dates automatically choose weekday/weekend profiles, and a calendar icon stays accessible when minimized. On narrow screens, the date sits beneath the clock. Dates affect typical traffic profiles and daylight; avoid implying historical traffic observations or calibrated seasonal demand.

Keep toolbox and weather buttons in a fixed left rail with panels opening beside them; only one of these panels can be open at a time. Weather uses independent relative-intensity rain and snow sliders with mixed precipitation and a clear reset. Preserve the lightweight map rendering, disable precipitation animation for reduced motion, and distinguish visual weather from traffic-model effects.

Weather should become more dramatic with 3D pitch through particle size, contrast, and layered motion, keeping the existing particle budget and 30 fps cap. Very heavy rain may add occasional lightning in tilted views: pure-white bolts with white radiance and bright white illumination, randomly alternating single and double pulses within a strike. Keep separate strikes at least eight seconds apart, with no flashes under reduced motion. Avoid blur, extra map redraws, or replacing native map lighting for storm effects.

Weather includes Current Toronto and Manual source modes. Current Toronto follows the clock's Current time mode, shows the model source, timestamp and physical rain/snow rates, and refreshes every 15 minutes. Moving a slider switches to Manual; leaving Current time freezes the current values as a manual scenario. Preserve manual choices against delayed responses and retain the last values on network failure. Rain above 85% triggers lightning consistently in either source mode.
